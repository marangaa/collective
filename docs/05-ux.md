# 05, UX and interface

## Who uses this

1. A community member on a cheap Android phone, on 3G or worse, more comfortable in Swahili, maybe standing in front of the building in question. They want to know if their clinic was supposed to be finished, and they want to say what they see.
2. A ward investigator, CSO researcher, or journalist on a laptop. They want the full chain of evidence, and they want to take it with them.
3. A reviewer (us and our partners). They work the claim approval queue.
4. A judge or first time visitor. They get 90 seconds to understand the idea.

## The main screen: the case file

One idea organizes the page: the story of one place over time, the record against reality. On desktop the timeline scrolls on the left and the map sticks on the right and follows the story. On mobile they stack and the map folds away. One rule covers everything: tap anything and you see its source.

Top to bottom:

1. Header. Project name, ward, county, sector. Below it, five verdict chips for budget, award, payments, delivery, and current state. Green means corroborated, red contradicted, grey unverifiable, amber partial. Those colors are used for verdicts and nothing else. Underneath, a freshness line like "Record last verified 12 days ago".
2. The tension summary. Two sentences built from the verdicts, for example: "The county paid KSh 165.1M for Phase II. Three audits and a 2025 re-tender indicate it was never completed." Record against reality, plainly said.
3. Timeline. Vertical, grouped by stage: budgeted, tendered, awarded, paid, claimed, audited, observed. Each node has a date, a one line claim, and a marker for its lane (official, independent, community). Open a node and you get its evidence card.
4. Map. The four facilities as pins colored by current verdict, the ward outline, field reports as clustered dots. Scrolling the timeline moves the map to the facility in focus. Slow connection or offline: the map folds into a list. It helps, it never blocks.
5. Evidence card. The claim first, then the exact quote, document title, page, publisher, retrieval date, a short hash, and a link to the original. This card is the heart of the product. Do not trust us, check.
6. Contradictions. Pairs side by side ("KSh 165,099,105 paid" next to "site abandoned since March 2023"), each with its documents behind it. The engine finds them. The reader decides.
7. What is missing. Gap cards like "No completion certificate in the public record." Each gap opens into a next step: which office, why them, and a pre-filled request under the Access to Information Act that you can view, copy, or download. This is the brief's "clear next steps", made literal.
8. Community reports. Newest first, each showing its corroboration state ("3 independent reports in the last month"), photos, and the prompt: "Were you there? Add what you saw."
9. Export. The whole case file as a PDF or JSON bundle with hashes, made for the investigator walking into a county assembly committee room.

## Filing a report (mobile first)

1. Which place? Nearby projects sorted by GPS distance, or pick from a list.
2. What did you see? Four big buttons: working, partly built, stalled or abandoned, never started. No typing needed.
3. Photo, optional. Uses the camera. Shrunk and stripped of location data on the phone before it goes anywhere (phone photos usually carry GPS, we remove it before upload).
4. Note, optional. A short line of text.
5. Send. It saves at once: "Saved. Will send when you are back online." A small chip shows pending or sent. The anonymous session is created silently on first use. No signup, no name, no phone number.

## Review console (reviewers only)

A queue of candidate claims, each next to its source page. Approve, edit, or reject, with keyboard shortcuts for speed. A button re-runs reconciliation. Run stats show tokens, cost, and pass rates, which is how we watch our own model.

## Landing page

One screen with the hook: "The county said it was built. Was it?" Then live case stats, then the case file. Then how verification works in three small diagrams: sources, evidence chain, action. A judge should get the thesis in one scroll.

## What offline promises

- Reading a case: the last viewed case is cached by the service worker, with an honest banner about its age.
- Map: optional layer, the list is always there.
- Reporting: fully offline, queue now and sync later.
- Photos: shrunk before upload, sent in small parts.
- API: batched calls, small payloads, cached at the edge.

Background Sync exists only in Chromium browsers. iOS Safari does not have it, so sync also runs on the online event, on app open, and through a visible retry button. We say that in the interface, not in a footnote.

## Language and access

All interface copy goes through one dictionary in English and Swahili from day one. Claim summaries use plain words. Money shows in KSh. Body text is at least 16px. Verdict colors always pair with icons, never color alone. The timeline works by keyboard.
